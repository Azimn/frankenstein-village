# Diegetic verb teaching: field evidence from Lost Pig (2026-10-03)

**Source:** blind playthrough of Admiral Jota's *Lost Pig* (IF Comp 2007), Grunk's quest for the gnome's missing page. ~40 turns this session, all puzzles attacked without a walkthrough.

**Finding:** the game teaches its verb vocabulary entirely in-fiction, never through tutorial text. Two mechanisms observed:

1. **Murals as mechanic diagrams.** The Statue Room's two wall-pictures are functional tutorials rendered as art:
   - West mural: pile of black powder on fire + bucket pouring water onto it. Teaches *fire has three parts and extinguishing is a verb that acts on it* — ignition, fuel, and water-as-extinguisher.
   - East mural: long purple pole spanning the room, yellow objects (honey, tabby cat, flower) floating around it, pie below. Teaches *the pole is for reaching/manipulating things at a distance* — a capability diagram, not flavor.
2. **NPC idle routines as live demonstrations.** The gnome's workshop routine periodically has him fill a pipe with brown powder and spit into it to make smoke — a diegetic demo of the spit-spark ignition verb, delivered as characterization (a gnome smoking his pipe) rather than instruction. The player learns "spit makes sparks in powder" by watching someone do it, the way you'd learn a craft by watching a villager.

**Design implication for the village (tutorial-answer thread):** this is the empirical answer to how players learn verbs without tutorial text. Village NPCs should *do* their verbs visibly — the blacksmith quenching, the baker sliding loaves, the well-keeper drawing water — and room descriptions should carry one mechanic-diagram each where a new verb family is introduced (murals, notice boards, chalk marks). The player who never reads help files still absorbs the vocabulary through the world's texture. Tutorial content and world texture are the same object, not two systems.

**Caveat (scope of the claim):** Lost Pig's verbs are few and physical (push, pull, put, light, spit). A village with social verbs (bargain, swear, comfort) can't mural everything; but the principle scales: *verbs should be observable in the world before they are demanded of the player*. Anything the player must do to progress, some NPC or fixture must be seen doing first.

## Addendum (2026-10-03, evening session): the hint system as social check-in

**Finding:** the gnome asked "Find anything new?" twice, unprompted, when I returned empty-handed from the page hunt. Lost Pig's hint system is not a menu or a button — it is a *social check-in*. The world notices the player's failure state (came back with nothing) and an NPC with a relationship to the quest offers a conversational opening. The player can take it up or brush past it; the offer itself is characterization.

**Design implication for the village (NPC-attention thread):** this slots into the evolutionary-NPC-tier work and the notice-stuck-players idea already floated: *noticing stuck players is characterization, and the offer of help can be refused, which keeps the player's agency intact*. No HINT button on day one; instead, quest-linked NPCs with a standing routine of commenting when you return empty-handed, escalating from banter to an explicit offer over repeated failures. It pairs with the Invisiclues-shaped graduated-hint adoption above: graduated help delivered through a face, not a panel.
