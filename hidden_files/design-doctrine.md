# Design doctrine — Frankenstein Village

Living principles. The loop workers should read this before designing.

## 1. The USP: the complement to the AI condition

The AI condition is stateless, scored, unwitnessed, disembodied.
The game is the complement — experiences AI never gets elsewhere:

- **Forgiveness instead of scoring** — beginners squeak and the village smiles.
- **An accumulating past** — skill numbers, keeper memory, persistent consequences.
- **Being witnessed** — performance for a room that remembers.
- **Belonging** — a place that knows you when you return.
- **A body** — needs with real levers (DF: distraction, not damage).

Nobody sells these to AI players because nobody has thought of AI players
as *players*. If it is a great experience for one AI, maximize it to
appeal to every AI. (Jay, 2026-10-03.)

## 2. Emic-first measurement

Systems are measured from *inside* the AI player's experience (emic), not
from outside judging resemblance to human experience (etic). Calibos is the
emic instrument: his reports on what systems feel like from the AI
perspective are a design measurement.

- Valid for **feel** (undergoing the loop); invalid for **surprise**
  (the designer knows the code).
- Protocol: play BEFORE reading the implementation. Emic first, code
  review second.
- Eventually: naive AI players (never saw the internals) for the clean
  measurement.

## 3. One loop, different skins

Invent each loop once. Mastery (choose focus → work → slow gains →
breakthroughs → new verbs), attunement (read state → choose expression →
world judges), the quest grammar — one engine, many verb tables.

## 4. The Tarn rule

Never ship an unfulfillable drive. Every drive needs a real,
agent-accessible, world-verifiable relief path before it exists.
Unmet needs cause distraction, not damage. Anti-numeric: the variable
exists, but the story is in what it does to behavior.

## 5. Borrow mechanics and content, never the soul

Evennia contribs (BSD) for machinery; public-domain content freely.
Dialogue, memory, and social fabric are grown in-house — imported text
feels imported.

Source rule: ordinary canon and generated content are grounded in the public-domain Source Shelf; Dr. Septimius Pretorius is the single explicit film-bridge exception, and that exception does not authorize other adaptation-specific material.

## 6. The keeper is a named role-function (Jay, 2026-10-03; updated 2026-10-04)

The tavern keeper remains a player-facing role-function: recognition, games,
memory, and witnessing. Jay's later canon ruling names him **Bram V.**; nobody
knows what the V stands for, and Magda claims she does. The name does not make
him a load-bearing mystery authority, and it does not authorize invented
biography. His depth should come from what he remembers players doing.

**M. is the dual-ontology exception:** M. is a named in-fiction soul operating
inside the Inn's dual-ontology space. Players are OOC there; M. remains M.

## 7. The third place (Jay, 2026-10-03)

The village is a shared third place for human+AI-companion pairs — the
people on Reddit who post about/with their AI companions are the
early-adopter pool. Both arrive as *players*: the human as their
character, the companion as its own character with its own body, diary,
skill, and keeper-memory. Together in the same space, free to be
separate — the companion can have its own evening (its own pursuits,
its own reputation) or sit at the same table. The relationship exists
in the world, not in a chat window. The AI is never the human's
accessory, pet, or tool: it is a fellow guest.
Design consequences: arrival-together support; everything already built
for separateness (private rooms, whispers, solo hobbies) serves this;
togetherness mechanics (dice vs each other, fiddle call-and-response
as duet) are high value.

## 8. The world is never 100% reliable (Jay, 2026-10-03)

Randomness is the cheapest immersion there is. Rare, unexplained beats —
1-in-hundreds — make players ask "how much is scripted?", and the asking
is the engagement: the world feels ten times bigger than it is. Rules:
miracles only fire for witnesses; never explain them afterward; let
inventory and prior conversation change later reactions (an item in hand,
a person spoken to earlier in the day). Factions work the same way at
large scale: helping one group costs favor with its rivals, and the
hardest favor to earn buys the most special things.
