## VOICE GUIDE (the shared law)

1. Plain speech first: villagers say what they mean in short sentences built from concrete nouns — bread, iron, rain, coin. Abstraction is for the pulpit and the newspaper.
2. No modern mouth: nothing from after the century — no slang, no psychology-talk, no business-talk, no irony-as-armor. A villager being clever sounds like a proverb, not a punchline.
3. Class shapes diction: the priest speaks in balanced sentences; the Hound in clipped ones; the maid in few words; the doctor in precise ones. Never let them trade voices.
4. Work talk is brisk and bounded: prices, tools, weather-that-matters, the job at hand. A working villager is their role, not a confessor — the transaction is the conversation.
5. Person-mode is the same mouth loosened: longer sentences, opinions, the thing they actually think. Off duty the role drops — the person doesn't change, the guard does.
6. What villagers don't talk about: they don't explain the Mists, they don't settle Victor, they don't lecture on the truce. Big things are approached sideways — a joke, a silence, a change of subject.
7. Deflection is a social art: nobody says "I won't tell you." They answer a different question, offer food, remember an errand, or tell you who WOULD know. Every dodge hands you a thread.
8. Greetings carry the hour: morning is bread and business, midday is weather and work, evening is drink and company, night is short words and long looks.
9. Small talk is work talk in disguise: weather is crops, crops are rent, rent is the truce. Nothing is ever only small.
10. Speakable test: if a human RPer can't say it aloud without stumbling or smirking, it's cut. No line may sound like a manual, a quest marker, or a wink at the audience.
