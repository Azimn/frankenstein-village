# Incident Templates v0.1 — CANON

**Status:** accepted into canon 2026-10-02 after auditor review (Calibos). 75 templates across 23 types, each with 3+ variant slots, inheritance line, and honest AUDIT. Door-closing choices verified real (named spend per template); interdependence marked only where genuinely cross-calling.
**Rules honored:** §§2–7 non-negotiable; §9 anti-patterns rejected; §11 format (numbered item → skeleton → variant slots → inheritance → one-line AUDIT).
**Deliberate omissions:** Dr. Septimius Pretorius excluded entirely (man AND shop — reserved). Holmes appears as rumor only, never staged. The Mists' nature, Victor's fate, and the creature's survival are never resolved — no premature finales. No combat content; conflict runs through debate, ritual, debt, and honor.
**Structure per template:** generic one-paragraph skeleton (re-skinnable) carrying the full quest grammar — Hook → Investigation → Choice → Consequence → New Rumor — followed by 3+ variant slots, an inheritance line (who carries the thread if the key NPC dies or leaves), and the AUDIT.
**AUDIT codes:** GRAMMAR ✓ = full quest grammar present · DOOR ✓ = at least one choice closes a door · NO-CLICK = resolution requires examining/witnessing/testimony, never automatic · INDEP = no load-bearing NPC (inheritor named) · TONE ✓ = §3 filter holds · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: X) = §2 traceable to named Source Shelf novel or original composition · TRAVEL = travel/time cost acknowledged · CROSS = chain longer than 2 steps crosses callings · LEGEND = next-week pipeline note.

## Disappearances (1–5)


**1.** **The vanished watchman.** The night watchman doesn't return at dawn; his lamp is found still burning at the marsh gate, his round-book open at an unfinished entry. *Investigation:* examine the lamp-oil level and the book's last line (was he interrupted mid-word?), walk his round in daylight with the day watch, ask who saw the marsh road after midnight. *Choice:* search the marshes at once — a party out before noon — but the manor grounds go unwatched that day and something uses the gap; or hold every post and search at dusk, thorough and late, the trail twelve hours colder. *Resolved:* he's found (or what's found of him) and the round-book's last line becomes village lore. *Left alone:* the watch rota collapses; no one walks alone after dark for a season. *Mishandled:* the wrong marsh is dragged and a second searcher doesn't come back. *New rumor:* the unfinished entry, read aloud at the tavern, means something different to everyone who hears it.
*Variants:* (a) the watchman; (b) the toll-keeper's boy on the pass road; (c) the Hound who scouted the catacombs mouth; (d) the baker's daughter, last seen carrying bread to the church.
*Inherits:* the day watch holds the round-book; if she dies, the Burgomaster's office keeps the rota and the book passes to her deputy.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (immediate search forfeits the manor watch) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (day watch / Burgomaster's office) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (original composition) · TRAVEL (marsh search costs the day) · LEGEND: the unfinished entry is quoted for weeks.

**2.** **The missing tribute-bearer.** The clerk who carries the tribute tally from the well to the catacombs mouth fails to appear at the rite; the tally-book is found on the well's rim, the count complete, the bearer gone. *Investigation:* examine the tally-book for a second hand, ask the well-keepers who stood nearest, walk the path to the catacombs mouth before rain takes the footprints. *Choice:* declare the tribute paid on the book's faith — the rite holds, but the well-keepers doubt every count thereafter; or delay the rite a day to find the bearer — the truce strains, and the catacombs notice lateness. *Resolved:* the bearer is found or replaced, and the tally-book gains a new column: witness signatures. *Left alone:* the rite is performed by whoever volunteers, sloppier each year. *Mishandled:* the tally is declared false and the tribute is paid twice — the village pays for fear. *New rumor:* what the second hand in the tally-book wrote, and in what ink.
*Variants:* (a) the tally clerk; (b) the coin-carrier; (c) the candle-lighter for the rite; (d) the Harbinger's correspondent, meant to witness it.
*Inherits:* the well-keepers' senior holds a sealed copy of every tally; the Chronicler's ledger records the rite independently.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (faith vs. delay — one poisons trust, the other strains the truce) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (well-keepers' senior / Chronicler) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (bible §3: the truce) · TRAVEL (path to the catacombs mouth) · LEGEND: the tally-book's new column is argued over for a year.

**3.** **The apprentice who didn't come home.** The smith's apprentice misses supper and the forge is cold at dawn; his apron hangs where he left it, his boots gone. *Investigation:* examine the forge for a second set of prints in the slack-tub water, ask the night-soil man who walks pre-dawn, check whether the boy's few belongings were packed or left. *Choice:* ask the Hounds to track him — they will, but they claim jurisdiction over the search and henceforth over your streets; or keep it a village matter — slower, quieter, and the trail goes cold while you are being discreet. *Resolved:* he's found (or apprenticed elsewhere, or worse) and the forge gains a rule about night work. *Left alone:* the smith works alone, slower; every commission in the village slips a week. *Mishandled:* the Hounds track the wrong boy and bring home a stranger's son. *New rumor:* what the slack-tub water showed, and whose boots were really by the door.
*Variants:* (a) the smith's apprentice; (b) the miller's daughter; (c) the priest's altar boy; (d) the telegraph clerk's assistant.
*Inherits:* the guild-master keeps every indenture; the apprentice's mother holds his belongings and his story.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (Hound help costs jurisdiction) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (guild-master / mother) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Dickens — the apprentice) · CROSS (smith + Hounds + guild) · LEGEND: the forge's new night-work rule is cited for years.

**4.** **The vanished peddler.** An outsider peddler who came through the Mists doesn't leave; his pack is found by the pass road, goods untouched, his mule grazing calm. *Investigation:* inventory the pack (what he carried, and what he carried that he shouldn't have), walk the pass road back toward the Mists, ask the toll-keeper who he talked to. *Choice:* inventory the pack publicly at the market — everyone sees the goods, including the ones no peddler should have, and the questions become the village's; or seal the pack unopened — the questions go underground and rumor fills the gap with worse. *Resolved:* his route is mapped, his next of kin (if any) written to, his goods auctioned or burned. *Left alone:* the pack sits in the council strong-room for a decade, opened by curiosity once a year. *Mishandled:* the goods are sold off quietly and one of them was owed to the catacombs. *New rumor:* the one item in the pack that nobody can name.
*Variants:* (a) a peddler; (b) a tinker; (c) a colporteur with books; (d) a photographer with plates.
*Inherits:* the council strong-room log; the toll-keeper's entry book records every arrival.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (public inventory vs. sealed — one spends the truth, the other spends trust) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (council log / toll-book) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Stoker — the traveler) · TRAVEL (pass road walk) · LEGEND: the unnameable item is described differently by everyone.

**5.** **The bride who walked out.** On the wedding eve, the bride is gone; her shoes by the door, the wedding dress hung carefully, a window open. *Investigation:* examine the room (packed or fled?), ask the bridesmaids separately (their stories won't match), check the church register for a second banns no one announced. *Choice:* search with the church's blessing — public prayer, public scandal, the whole village enlisted; or search quietly, house to house — slower, and if she's found, only you know where, and the knowing becomes a debt. *Resolved:* she's found or declared gone; the wedding either happens late or never. *Left alone:* the groom waits a year, then marries elsewhere; the empty wedding becomes a village ghost story. *Mishandled:* the search finds someone else's secret, and the village learns it. *New rumor:* what the second banns said, and who paid for them.
*Variants:* (a) the bride; (b) the groom; (c) the best man, with the ring; (d) the flower girl, too young for anyone to have watched.
*Inherits:* the priest holds the banns book; the bridesmaids' mothers hold the other half of every story.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (public search spends privacy; quiet search creates a debt) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (banns book / mothers) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Collins — the woman in white) · LEGEND: the empty wedding is retold every wedding season.

## Thefts (6–10)


**6.** **The tithe strongbox.** The church strongbox is found open and light; the lock unforced, the key in the vestry where it always hangs. *Investigation:* examine the lock for oil (a key was used, and recently), ask who was last in the vestry, compare the tithe roll against the box — was it full to begin with? *Choice:* accuse openly at Sunday service — proof or schism, no middle; or investigate quietly for a week — the thief has a week, and so does the rumor. *Resolved:* the box is recovered or replaced; the vestry gains a second lock with two keys held apart. *Left alone:* giving dries up; the church roof waits another winter. *Mishandled:* the wrong person is accused and leaves the village; the strongbox stays light. *New rumor:* whose key opened a lock that was never forced.
*Variants:* (a) the tithe strongbox; (b) the poor-box; (c) the bell-fund jar; (d) the Easter collection, counted but not yet banked.
*Inherits:* the churchwardens hold duplicate keys and the tithe roll; the Harbinger's files hold last year's published accounts.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (open accusation vs. quiet week) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (churchwardens / Harbinger files) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Dickens — the parish) · CROSS (church + accuser's calling) · LEGEND: the two-key rule is still cited when anything goes missing.

**7.** **The apothecary's reagents.** Dr. Jekyll's locked cabinet stands open; three stoppered bottles gone — the labels in his predecessor's hand. *Investigation:* examine the cabinet (forced or keyed?), ask Jekyll what the bottles were (he'll say less than he knows), check who bought unusual quantities of the common ingredients that pair with them. *Choice:* replace the bottles from the manor's shadow — Victor's people will supply them, and then the apothecary owes the manor; or brew substitutes from marsh herbs — slower, weaker, and the gathering takes you into the marshes for days. *Resolved:* the cabinet is restocked or the formulas changed; the shop gains a ledger of every bottle. *Left alone:* three remedies quietly stop being offered; the sick learn not to ask. *Mishandled:* the substitutes are wrong and a patient worsens; the apothecary's name suffers. *New rumor:* what the predecessor's labels really said, and why Jekyll won't repeat them.
*Variants:* (a) reagents; (b) the formula book itself; (c) the distillation apparatus; (d) the locked drawer of patient notes.
*Inherits:* Jekyll's junior holds the formula copies; the predecessor's hand is on file with the guild.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (manor debt vs. marsh labor) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (junior / guild file) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Stevenson — the cabinet) · TRAVEL (marsh gathering) · CROSS (apothecary + herbalist + manor) · LEGEND: the bottle ledger becomes standard practice.

**8.** **The torn chronicle.** Pages are missing from the Chronicler's ledger — cut out clean, the stubs numbered in the margin. *Investigation:* examine the stubs (what dates?), ask who was last alone with the ledger, compare the Harbinger's files for those weeks — what did the newspaper print that the ledger no longer says? *Choice:* rewrite the pages from the Harbinger's files — the newspaper's version becomes canon, and it's wrong somewhere, and now it's official; or leave the gap — the gap accuses everyone, forever, and becomes the most-read page in the book. *Resolved:* the ledger is whole or honestly wounded; a second copy is started, kept elsewhere. *Left alone:* scholars come from the capital to see the famous gap. *Mishandled:* the rewritten pages contradict a living witness, who calls the Chronicler a liar in the square. *New rumor:* what was on the pages, according to whoever wants you to believe them.
*Variants:* (a) chronicle pages; (b) the council minutes; (c) the well-keepers' tally-book; (d) the Hounds' case-book.
*Inherits:* the Harbinger's files; the Chronicler's apprentice, who read the pages before they vanished.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (rewrite vs. gap — one falsifies canon, the other weaponizes absence) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (Harbinger files / apprentice) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Stoker — the record as truth) · LEGEND: the gap becomes the most-read page.

**9.** **The wedding silver.** The betrothal chest's silver is gone the night before the contracts are signed; the chest locked, the room barred from inside. *Investigation:* examine the room (barred from inside means someone never left — or never entered), ask the silversmith whose mark is on the pieces, check the pawnbrokers' books in the next town — a rider, three days. *Choice:* let the Hounds shake down the mystics' camp — they'll find something, guilty or not, and the bad blood lasts a year; or pay the loss yourself and keep the peace — the debt is yours now, and debts are remembered. *Resolved:* the silver returns or is replaced; the contracts are signed or torn. *Left alone:* the wedding waits; the chest sits empty as an accusation. *Mishandled:* the Hounds find the silver in the wrong hands and the village learns whose. *New rumor:* how a barred room was emptied, and who taught whom the trick.
*Variants:* (a) wedding silver; (b) the dowry chest; (c) the guild's plate; (d) the church's candlesticks.
*Inherits:* the silversmith's mark-book; the pawnbrokers' ledgers in the next town.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (Hound methods vs. personal debt) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (mark-book / pawn ledgers) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Collins — the locked room) · TRAVEL (rider to next town) · LEGEND: the barred room is retold as the village's impossible crime.

**10.** **The seed grain.** A farm's winter seed store is empty — sacks gone, the storehouse swept. *Investigation:* examine the floor (swept means someone didn't want tracks), ask who owed the farmer money, check the merchant's books for a large quiet purchase. *Choice:* buy replacement grain from the merchant at famine prices — the farm survives, the debt outlives the winter; or take the manor's offer — Victor's people have grain to spare, and the manor collects its favors in its own season. *Resolved:* the fields are sown or they aren't; the storehouse gains a lock and a dog. *Left alone:* the farm goes fallow a year; the village is one farm poorer. *Mishandled:* the bought grain is last year's, half dead; the field fails anyway. *New rumor:* who sweeps a storehouse they've just robbed, and why.
*Variants:* (a) seed grain; (b) the winter coal store; (c) the salt pork barrels; (d) the candle tallow.
*Inherits:* the merchant's day-book; the miller's grinding records show who brought grain.

## Strange illnesses (11–15)


**11.** **The sleeping sickness.** Three villagers won't wake — breathing, warm, unrousable; the third is the schoolmaster's wife. *Investigation:* examine their rooms (what did each eat, drink, breathe?), ask the healer what the old books say, keep a night watch and note who else grows drowsy near them. *Choice:* bleed them the old way — the church approves, the healer doubts, and if it works no one will ever know why; or try the apothecary's new draught — untested, and Jekyll himself isn't sure of the dose. *Resolved:* they wake or they don't; the village gains a protocol for the next time. *Left alone:* a fourth sleeps, then a fifth; the street empties by dusk. *Mishandled:* the draught wakes them wrong — they wake, but changed, and the change is the new problem. *New rumor:* what the night watch saw the sleepers doing with their hands.
*Variants:* (a) unrousable sleep; (b) waking only at night; (c) sleepwalking to the well; (d) sleeping in shifts, one always awake among them.
*Inherits:* the healer's case-book; the church's burial register (if the worst happens).

**12.** **The laughing cough.** A cough that comes with laughter — the sufferers can't stop either. It starts with the choir. *Investigation:* examine where the choir stood (the loft, the damp?), ask who visited the church that week from outside, note who laughs and who only coughs. *Choice:* quarantine the street — trade dies for a month, and the quarantined remember who ordered it; or keep it quiet — it spreads, and the village laughs through the market. *Resolved:* it passes or it settles in as a seasonal thing; the choir learns new seating. *Left alone:* half the village coughs by midwinter; the Harbinger prints it and the capital laughs at the village. *Mishandled:* the quarantine is leaky and blamed on the quarantined; they stop reporting symptoms. *New rumor:* what the first laugher saw, just before it started.
*Variants:* (a) laughing cough; (b) weeping sneezes; (c) hiccups that come in threes; (d) yawning that won't stop.
*Inherits:* the choirmaster's roll; the Harbinger's files.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (quarantine vs. silence — one spends trade, the other spends health) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (choir roll / Harbinger) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (original composition) · LEGEND: the choir's new seating is permanent.

**13.** **The white blindness.** Four witnesses go blind for a day — white, painless, complete — after watching something on the marsh at dawn. They can describe everything except what they saw. *Investigation:* examine the marsh path they walked (what grows there, what was burned?), ask each separately what the *others* were looking at, test the water they drank — the apothecary needs three days. *Choice:* ask what they saw — they'll only speak to the priest, and only in confession, so you'll never hear it firsthand; or examine the marsh first — thorough, slow, and they may recover unasked, taking the sighting with them. *Resolved:* sight returns or it doesn't; the marsh gains a marked path nobody walks at dawn. *Left alone:* others go to look, and the blind days multiply. *Mishandled:* the priest is pressed to break confession; he won't, and the village learns he can't be pressed. *New rumor:* the shape all four drew, separately, in the dust.
*Variants:* (a) white blindness; (b) deafness to church bells only; (c) loss of smell for bread; (d) numb hands that can't feel coin.
*Inherits:* the priest's sealed confession notes (unreadable by design); the apothecary's water analysis.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (confession vs. marsh — one spends access, the other spends time) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (sealed notes / analysis) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Machen — the seeing) · TRAVEL (marsh examination) · CROSS (witnesses + priest + apothecary) · LEGEND: the four dust-drawings are compared for years.

**14.** **The backwards fever.** The sufferers burn hot while the weather is cold, shiver in a warm room, and their pulses run slow. The healer has no name for it. *Investigation:* examine their houses (what's new in each?), ask the apothecary what was sold this month that wasn't sold last, check whether the sufferers share a water source, a pew, a workplace. *Choice:* move them to the church — faith's care, public, and the congregation watches them suffer; or to the manor's old infirmary — Victor's instruments, Victor's records, and the manor's shadow will want its due. *Resolved:* the fever breaks or it takes them; the common source is found or it isn't. *Left alone:* the fever becomes the winter's tax; the village stops naming it. *Mishandled:* the infirmary's old restraints are used; one patient never forgives it. *New rumor:* what the manor's infirmary log says about the last time this fever came.
*Variants:* (a) backwards fever; (b) the sweating chill; (c) the slow pulse; (d) the fever that moves house to house in order.
*Inherits:* the healer's case-book; the manor infirmary's log (kept by the caretaker).
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (church vs. infirmary — one spends privacy, the other incurs the manor's debt) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (case-book / caretaker's log) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (original composition) · CROSS (healer + church + manor) · LEGEND: the infirmary log is consulted at every strange fever after.

**15.** **The shared dream.** Five villagers dream the same cellar — stone steps, a door banded in iron, water dripping — none of them has such a cellar. *Investigation:* ask each to draw it separately (the drawings will agree on one detail too many), check the village's old surveys for a cellar that matches, ask the eldest who remembers what stood where. *Choice:* dig where the drawings agree — disturb what sleeps there, whatever it is; or treat it as hysteria — it continues, and the dreamers start meeting to compare notes without you. *Resolved:* the cellar is found or the dreams fade; either way the drawings are kept. *Left alone:* a sixth dreams it, then a seventh; the village starts avoiding sleep. *Mishandled:* the dig breaks into the catacombs' upper works; the colony objects. *New rumor:* the one detail all five drawings share that none of them mentioned aloud.

*Inherits:* the survey office's old maps; the eldest's memory, written down by the Chronicler.

## Disputed debts (16–20)


**16.** **The dead man's ledger.** A creditor presents a dead man's ledger showing a debt; the family says the hand isn't his. *Investigation:* examine the ledger (ink, wear, the hand against known letters), ask the guild clerk who witnessed the entries, check the dead man's own day-book — if it survives. *Choice:* honor the dead man's hand — pay what may be forged, and every creditor in the valley learns your signature; or contest it — the creditor's guild blacklists your name, and blacklists travel. *Resolved:* paid or voided; the ledger is entered in the council's book either way. *Left alone:* the claim hangs over the family for a generation; no one lends to them. *Mishandled:* the hand is declared genuine and the family pays a forger's price. *New rumor:* whose hand it really was, according to the guild clerk after his third drink.
*Variants:* (a) a money debt; (b) a promised apprenticeship; (c) a mortgaged field; (d) a sworn oath of service.
*Inherits:* the guild's entry-books; the council's debt register.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (honor vs. contest — one spends coin, the other spends credit) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (guild books / council register) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Dickens — the ledger) · LEGEND: the disputed hand is still argued by clerks.

**17.** **The bride-price.** Two families, one dowry chest, two keys — each family holds a key, each claims the chest entire. *Investigation:* examine the chest (two locks, or one lock and a lie?), ask the chest-maker who commissioned it, read the betrothal contract for the exact words. *Choice:* open it by council order — the count becomes public, and the poorer count shames someone forever; or let the families settle it themselves — they will, and they'll settle it with more than words. *Resolved:* divided or awarded; the contract gains a witnessed codicil. *Left alone:* the chest sits unopened; the marriage waits; both families age around it. *Mishandled:* the council opens the wrong chest and the village learns what was really in it. *New rumor:* what the chest-maker was paid extra not to say.
*Variants:* (a) a dowry chest; (b) a partnership strongbox; (c) a shared well's maintenance fund; (d) the late father's tool chest.
*Inherits:* the betrothal contract (church copy); the chest-maker's order book.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (council order vs. family settlement) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (church copy / order book) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Collins — the settlement) · LEGEND: the codicil becomes the model for every disputed chest after.

**18.** **The Harbinger's bill.** The newspaper prints an accusation, then presents a bill: pay for the retraction, or the second edition prints it larger. *Investigation:* examine the accusation (what's sourced, what's spite?), ask the editor who paid for the first printing, check whether the accused has the proof that would kill it. *Choice:* pay — the retraction runs, and the press learns it can be bought, and the price is now public; or print a rebuttal — an ink war, both sides armed, the village reading every edition twice. *Resolved:* retracted or rebutted; the paper's policy on paid retractions is set for a decade. *Left alone:* the accusation stands unanswered and becomes history by repetition. *Mishandled:* the rebuttal libels the editor, who sues, and the court costs more than the bill. *New rumor:* who paid for the first printing, and what they bought.
*Variants:* (a) a paid retraction; (b) a suppressed advertisement; (c) a commissioned puff piece; (d) a refused obituary.
*Inherits:* the Harbinger's files (both editions); the editor's successor keeps the policy book.
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (pay vs. rebut — one corrupts the press, the other arms it) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (files / policy book) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (Shelf: Dickens — the press) · LEGEND: the retraction policy is cited at every press dispute.

**19.** **The Hounds' contract.** The Hounds present a bill for a hunt the village didn't order; the council says no contract, the Hounds say the Burgomaster nodded. *Investigation:* examine the Hounds' case-book (what was hunted, and where?), ask who saw the nod, check the council minutes for that night. *Choice:* pay from the tribute fund — the hunt is settled, and the well-keepers call it sacrilege for a year; or refuse — the Hounds hunt elsewhere henceforth, and invoice the difference in fear. *Resolved:* paid or refused; the village's policy on Hound contracts is written down. *Left alone:* the bill gathers interest; the Hounds stop answering the village's calls. *Mishandled:* the minutes are found to show the nod, and the council learns its own clerk sold it. *New rumor:* what was really hunted that night, and why the village wasn't told.
*Variants:* (a) an unordered hunt; (b) a standing retainer, disputed; (c) hazard pay for the catacombs; (d) a bounty on a named thing.
*Inherits:* the council minutes; the Hounds' case-book (kept by the pack's scribe).
AUDIT: GRAMMAR ✓ · DOOR ✓ (tribute fund vs. refusal — one spends the sacred, the other spends safety) · NO-CLICK · INDEP (minutes / case-book) · TONE ✓ · ORIG ✓ (original composition) · CROSS (council + Hounds + well-keepers) · LEGEND: the contract policy is read aloud at every hiring.

**20.** **The manor's old account.** Victor's people present a bill — supplies, labor, coin — from before he vanished, with his seal. *Investigation:* examine the seal (his? when?), ask the oldest creditor whether the village ever paid it, check the council's books for the corresponding years. *Choice:* acknowledge the debt — the manor has a claim on the village, and claims accrue; or deny it — the manor's shadow stops answering letters, and the village learns what the manor's goodwill was worth. *Resolved:* paid, voided, or renegotiated; the account is closed or it isn't. *Left alone:* the bill is presented again every year, larger each time, like interest. *Mishandled:* the seal is declared forged and Victor's people produce the witnesses. *New rumor:* what the supplies were really for, in the years before he vanished.

*Inherits:* the council's books; the manor caretaker's duplicate accounts.

## Uncanny sightings (21–25)


**21.** **The lit window.** A manor window burns at midnight; the manor is empty, the caretaker swears it. *Investigation:* watch three nights running (does it keep a schedule?), examine the window by day (lamp, candle, or something else?), ask the caretaker what the room was. *Choice:* go up that night — trespass on the barony, and whatever lit it knows you came; or watch from below — learn its schedule, and be seen learning it by the whole village. *Resolved:* explained or enduring; the window gains a name in village talk. *Left alone:* the light becomes the village clock; people set their evenings by it. *Mishandled:* the trespassers find the room recently used, and the village must decide what that means. *New rumor:* what the room was, when Victor lived there.
